*The best free perimenopause apps in 2026 are all free to download — Rythma (our app), Balance, Clue, Flo, Apple Health, Perry, and Caria — so you can log symptoms and see whether an app fits before spending anything. What differs is depth: some, like Rythma, are built specifically for the irregular cycles of perimenopause, while others began as general period trackers and added a midlife mode later. Perimenopause is the transition leading up to your final period. It usually begins in your mid- to late 40s and lasts about four years on average, sometimes as long as eight, according to the U.S. Office on Women's Health. During this stage, the same agency notes, periods may run longer or shorter, heavier or lighter, skip months entirely, and you may not ovulate every cycle. That unpredictability is exactly why a tracking app helps — and why "free" matters: you want to try an app long enough to see your own patterns before deciding whether it earns a subscription.

Every app below is **free to download** and lets you start tracking without paying; most also offer optional in-app purchases for deeper reports or content libraries. Pricing changes often, so we link to each app's own page rather than quoting figures. We also disclose up front that Rythma is our own app, and we have described every other tool fairly.

## What "free" actually gets you in a perimenopause app

Four things to check when comparing free tiers:

- **How much you can log for free.** Can you track the full range of perimenopause symptoms — hot flashes, night sweats, sleep, mood, brain fog, fatigue — without hitting a paywall? The NHS lists all of these as common.
- **Whether you can see your patterns.** Some apps let you log for free but reserve trend charts and insights for a paid tier.
- **Whether you get a doctor-ready summary.** A shareable symptom report is one of the most useful features for appointments; apps differ on whether it is free.
- **What "free" costs in ads or data.** It sometimes means ad-supported or funded by data sharing. Check the privacy policy.

No app wins on every line. Here is how the leading free-to-download options compare.

Rythma is our app, so we will be specific rather than just flattering. It is an iPhone app built specifically for perimenopause rather than adapted from a standard period tracker. It is free to download, and from the start you can log the symptoms that define this stage — fatigue, sleep disruption, mood shifts, hot flashes, brain fog — without assuming a fixed 28-day cycle. ## Balance — free education plus a health report

Balance, from Newson Health and founded by Dr. Louise Newson, is one of the most established menopause apps and is free to download. It pairs symptom tracking with a large library of evidence-based articles and a Health Report you can bring to appointments; according to its makers, Balance is NHS-recognized and certified by the digital-health assessor ORCHA. Where it shines for free is education and trust — a clinician-backed brand with deep written content alongside your tracking. Its focus is broad menopause rather than the prediction of specific hard days, and some features sit behind a paid tier.

**Best for:** women who want authoritative menopause education plus tracking and a shareable report without paying upfront. **Trade-offs:** broad focus rather than perimenopause-specific prediction. Confirm what is free versus paid on the [Balance app page](https://www.balance-menopause.com/balance-app/).

## Clue — a privacy-forward tracker with a free perimenopause mode

Clue is a long-running, science-forward period and cycle tracker with a strong privacy reputation, headquartered in Berlin and subject to strict EU data-protection law. It is free to download and includes a Perimenopause mode with a cycle view designed for changing cycle lengths, plus broad symptom tracking — a solid, trustworthy option if data privacy is a priority. Its origins are cycle-first, so its strongest instincts are around bleeding patterns and cycle changes rather than midlife prediction, and some features live in a paid Clue Plus tier.

**Best for:** privacy-conscious users who want a mature, science-led tracker with a dedicated perimenopause view. **Trade-offs:** hard-day prediction is not its focus. See its own explainer on the [Clue Perimenopause mode](https://helloclue.com/articles/menopause/introducing-clue-perimenopause).

## Flo — the largest tracker, with a free perimenopause experience

Flo is the largest general period and cycle tracker, and it is free to download. Its dedicated perimenopause experience includes a "Perimenopause Score" — which the company describes as the first digital assessment tool validated for perimenopause symptoms — along with symptom tracking, window-based next-period prediction, and an anonymous community. For free, it offers a polished experience and a very large user base. Like Clue, it began as a cycle-first app, so its next-period predictions can wobble when perimenopause scrambles the rhythm, and some insights require Flo Premium.

**Best for:** women who want a mainstream, feature-rich tracker with a community and a structured symptom assessment. **Trade-offs:** cycle-first origins; deeper features are paid. Read the company's own announcement about [Flo for Perimenopause](https://flo.health/newsroom/flo-for-perimenopause-is-launching-to-empower-the-1-billion-women-who-experience-perimenopause-without-the-support-they-deserve).

## Apple Health — free and already on your iPhone

If you have an iPhone, Apple Health's Cycle Tracking is free and already installed. You can log periods and some symptoms, and the data stays on your device with Apple's privacy protections. Its limits show up fast in perimenopause, though: it offers minimal perimenopause-specific intelligence — no hard-day prediction, no doctor report — so it captures data without doing much interpreting.

**Best for:** iPhone users who want free, no-frills logging with nothing new to install. **Trade-offs:** no perimenopause-specific prediction or shareable report.

## Perry and Caria — free community and AI companions

Two more midlife-focused apps round out the list, both free to download. **Perry** is built around peer support and community alongside tracking — part of what it offers is the sense of not going through this alone. **Caria** pairs symptom tracking with AI-assisted guidance. Both are designed for midlife rather than reproductive-age cycles.

**Best for:** women who value peer community (Perry) or AI guidance (Caria) alongside tracking. **Trade-offs:** feature sets and pricing change quickly; verify on each app's store listing.

## How to choose on the free angle

Download one or two free apps and log for a few weeks — long enough to catch a real pattern, since hormones in perimenopause fluctuate "like a rollercoaster" rather than declining smoothly, as the Cleveland Clinic puts it. Then pick on fit: Balance for education, Clue for privacy, Flo for community, Apple Health for the bare minimum, and Rythma to anticipate hard days and prep a clear doctor's summary.

Whatever you choose, an app is a tracking tool, not a diagnosis. If you have very heavy bleeding, bleeding between periods, or cycles consistently closer than about 21 days apart, the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists advises seeing a doctor.
