## Why irregular cycles break a standard period app

Perimenopause is the transition leading up to your final period. According to the U.S. Office on Women's Health, it usually starts in your mid- to late 40s and lasts about four years on average, sometimes up to eight — and its hallmark is that cycles become unpredictable. That unpredictability isn't random noise; clinicians measure it to stage the transition. Under the widely used STRAW+10 staging system, the early menopause transition begins when consecutive cycle lengths persistently differ by seven or more days, and the late transition is marked by a stretch of 60 or more days without a period. So a seven-day swing is a signal, not a glitch; a 60-day gap is a defined stage, not a "late" period.

This is exactly where a standard period tracker strains. These apps predict one next-period date from a stable average, so when your cycle swings by more than a week month to month, that average stops meaning much and a confidently wrong "your period is 12 days late" alert is worse than useless. The Cleveland Clinic describes perimenopausal hormones as fluctuating "like a rollercoaster" rather than declining smoothly — the biological reason the calendar stops cooperating. An app built for irregularity treats the swing as the signal; one built for regularity treats it as an error.

## Flo — window prediction for a moving target

Flo is the largest general period and cycle tracker, and it has built a dedicated perimenopause experience on that scale. According to Flo, it includes a Perimenopause Score — which the company describes as the first digital assessment tool validated for perimenopause symptoms — plus symptom tracking and an anonymous community.

For irregular periods, Flo's most useful move is shifting from a single predicted date to a prediction window — a range your next period is likely to fall in, rather than one date it will almost certainly miss. That is a sensible response to widening variability, and Flo's content library and polished interface make it easy to live in day to day. The honest caveat: Flo's roots are in reproductive-age cycle tracking, so its perimenopause experience is a layer on a cycle-first product.

**Best for** people who want the largest, most polished general tracker with a perimenopause layer and a prediction window. See [Flo's perimenopause announcement](https://flo.health/newsroom/flo-for-perimenopause-is-launching-to-empower-the-1-billion-women-who-experience-perimenopause-without-the-support-they-deserve).

## Clue — a cycle view built for changing lengths

Clue is a long-running, science-forward tracker with one of the stronger privacy reputations in the category — per its own materials it is built in Berlin and handles health data under strict EU data-protection rules. For irregular cycles, its relevant feature is a Perimenopause mode pairing perimenopause-specific symptom tracking with a cycle view built to adapt to changing lengths rather than assuming a fixed one.

That is a genuine, thoughtful response: instead of averaging your swings into a single misleading date, Clue's view reflects that your cycles are shifting. Like Flo, though, Clue began as a reproductive-age cycle tracker, so its center of gravity is still the cycle itself — it adapts to irregularity rather than forecasting which days will be hard.

**Best for** privacy-conscious users who want a science-forward tracker whose cycle view adapts to changing lengths. See [Clue's Perimenopause overview](https://helloclue.com/articles/menopause/introducing-clue-perimenopause).

The best fit is an app built for irregularity, not a fixed cycle. ### Why does my regular period app stop working in perimenopause?

Standard apps predict one next-period date from a stable average. In perimenopause, cycles run longer or shorter or skip months entirely, per the U.S. Office on Women's Health, so that average stops meaning much. Under STRAW+10, cycle lengths differing by seven or more days marks the early transition — the swing that breaks it.

### Is a 60-day gap between periods normal in perimenopause?

Longer gaps are recognized: STRAW+10 defines the late menopause transition by a stretch of 60 or more days without a period. Still, the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists advises seeing a clinician for very heavy bleeding, bleeding between periods or after sex, cycles consistently under about 21 days, or any bleeding after menopause.
