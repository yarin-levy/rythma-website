## Why perimenopause changes what you need from a tracker

Perimenopause is the stretch of hormonal change leading up to your final period. According to the U.S. Office on Women's Health, it usually begins in your mid- to late 40s and lasts about four years on average, sometimes up to eight. Its defining feature is unpredictability: the same agency notes that periods may run longer or shorter, heavier or lighter, skip months entirely, and that you may not ovulate every cycle, while the Cleveland Clinic describes the underlying hormones as behaving "like a rollercoaster" rather than declining smoothly. That is why a tool built around a tidy monthly cycle — in Stardust's case, one tied to the moon — strains in midlife.

The symptoms also broaden beyond bleeding. In an international survey of more than 17,000 women across 158 countries analyzed by The Menopause Society, fatigue was the most reported symptom at 83%, ahead of irritability (80%), depressive mood (77%), and sleep problems (76%); hot flashes were recognized as a perimenopause sign by only 71%. A good perimenopause app has to take all of that seriously, not just the calendar.

## What Stardust does well — and where it stops

Stardust's strengths are real for the right person: it is a polished, design-forward tracker, and for a regular reproductive-age cycle it does the core job — logging periods and symptoms and predicting the next period — in an interface many users enjoy. Where it stops is midlife. Its center of gravity is the monthly cycle, not the less rhythmic reality of perimenopause: it is not built to expect skipped and shifting cycles, to weigh fatigue, mood, and sleep as heavily as bleeding, or to warn you a hard stretch is coming. You can review Stardust's current features on its own site.

Rythma is our app, so we will be specific rather than just praising it. It is an iPhone app built specifically for perimenopause rather than adapted from a standard period tracker: instead of assuming a fixed monthly cycle, it expects irregularity and learns your individual symptom patterns over time. ## Balance — the alternative for clinician-backed education

If you are leaving Stardust because you want authoritative menopause education alongside tracking, Balance is a leading option. From Newson Health and founded by Dr. Louise Newson, it is one of the most established menopause apps, offering symptom tracking, a large library of evidence-based articles, and a Health Report you can take to appointments. According to its makers, it has been recognized by the NHS and certified by the digital-health assessor ORCHA. Where it shines is education and trust — a serious counterpart to Stardust's lighter feel — though its focus is broad menopause rather than predicting specific hard days.

Confirm current features on the [Balance app page](https://www.balance-menopause.com/balance-app/).

## Caria and Perry — the alternatives for AI guidance or community

Two more midlife-focused apps suit people who want something Stardust does not offer. **Caria** pairs symptom tracking with AI-assisted guidance, leaning toward answering questions alongside logging. **Perry** is built around peer support, combining tracking with a community of women going through the same stage — useful if part of what you want is to feel less alone. Both are built for midlife rather than reproductive-age cycles. Because both evolve quickly, check their listings first.

Feature sets and availability change quickly, so verify on each app's store listing.

## Clue — the alternative if you want a science-forward, privacy-first tracker

If you liked that Stardust is a modern tracker but want more rigor and less astrology, Clue is the natural comparison. It is a long-running, science-forward period tracker made in Berlin, with one of the better privacy reputations in the category and strong EU data protection. It also has a dedicated Clue Perimenopause mode with a Cycle View that adapts to changing cycle lengths. Being fair, Clue began as a reproductive-age tracker, so its center of gravity is still the cycle rather than predicting your hardest days — but it is a thoughtful step toward midlife.

See the [Clue Perimenopause overview](https://helloclue.com/articles/menopause/introducing-clue-perimenopause).

## Flo — the alternative if you want the largest general tracker

If leaving Stardust simply means wanting a bigger, better-resourced cycle app, Flo is the obvious comparison. It is the largest general period and cycle tracker and has added a dedicated perimenopause experience: according to Flo, this includes a Perimenopause Score — which the company describes as the first digital assessment tool validated for perimenopause symptoms — plus a prediction window for your next period rather than a single date, which suits irregular cycles better. But Flo, like Stardust, has roots in reproductive-age tracking, so perimenopause is a layer on a cycle-first product.

Details are on [Flo's perimenopause announcement](https://flo.health/newsroom/flo-for-perimenopause-is-launching-to-empower-the-1-billion-women-who-experience-perimenopause-without-the-support-they-deserve).

## How to choose a Stardust alternative

A simple way to decide, based on why you are looking:

- **To anticipate hard days and bring a clear report to your doctor:** a perimenopause-first app built for prediction, like Rythma.
- **For deep, clinician-backed education:** Balance.
- **For AI guidance or peer community:** Caria or Perry.
- **For a science-forward, privacy-first tracker with a perimenopause mode:** Clue.
- **For the largest general tracker with a perimenopause mode:** Flo.
- **If your cycle is still regular and you love the astrology framing:** you may not need to switch at all.

Whatever you pick, the value comes from consistent logging: patterns and predictions only emerge once you have enough data, so give it a few weeks.
