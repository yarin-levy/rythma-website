When people search for a "menopause app," they usually are not yet at menopause itself. Menopause is a single point in time — 12 consecutive months since your final period with no other medical cause, according to the World Health Organization. The symptoms come before that milestone, during perimenopause, which is defined by unpredictability: the U.S. Office on Women's Health notes periods may run longer or shorter, heavier or lighter, skip months entirely, and you may not ovulate every cycle, while hormones fluctuate "like a rollercoaster" rather than declining smoothly, as the Cleveland Clinic describes it. That reshapes which app helps, because most cycle trackers were built to predict a roughly 28-day rhythm — exactly what breaks down here.

We disclose up front that Rythma is our own app, describe every other tool fairly, and link to each app's own page for details that change, such as pricing.

## What to look for in a menopause app

A strong menopause app should:

- **Expect irregularity** — handle skipped, long, and short cycles without guessing a "next period" date it cannot support.
- **Track the right symptoms** — beyond bleeding, that means hot flashes, night sweats, sleep disruption, mood changes, brain fog, and fatigue.
- **Show your patterns** — surface trends and connect symptoms over time, not just store logs.
- **Help with doctor visits** — a clear, shareable summary makes appointments more productive.
- **Respect your privacy** — cycle and symptom data is sensitive, so check each app's practices.

Below, the menopause-specific apps come first, then the cycle trackers that added perimenopause features.

## Rythma — built for prediction and the unpredictability of perimenopause

Rythma is our app, so we will be specific rather than simply praise it. It is an iPhone app built for perimenopause from the start rather than adapted from a period tracker: instead of assuming a fixed monthly cycle, it expects irregularity and learns your symptom patterns over time.

Its distinguishing feature is prediction. As you log, Rythma identifies your patterns and forecasts difficult days — high-symptom stretches of fatigue, poor sleep, mood shifts, hot flashes, or brain fog — before they arrive, so you can plan around them. It also generates a shareable report of your symptoms and trends for appointments.

**Best for:** women in their 40s and early 50s with irregular periods who want to anticipate hard days and bring a clean summary to a doctor. **Trade-offs:** iPhone-only today, and prediction improves the longer you use it, so the first weeks are mostly logging. ## Balance — strongest on education and clinical credibility

Balance, from Newson Health and founded by Dr. Louise Newson, is one of the most established menopause apps. It offers symptom tracking, a large library of evidence-based articles, and a Health Report you can take to appointments. According to its makers, Balance is NHS-recognized and certified by the digital-health assessor ORCHA.

Where Balance shines is education and trust: if you want a clinician-backed brand with deep content about menopause and hormone health, it is a leading choice. Its focus is broad menopause rather than the prediction of specific hard days.

**Best for:** women who want authoritative menopause education alongside tracking and a report. Confirm current features and pricing on the [Balance app page](https://www.balance-menopause.com/balance-app/).

## Caria and Perry — companion and community options

Two more midlife-focused apps are worth knowing. **Caria** is a companion app that pairs symptom tracking with AI-assisted guidance. **Perry** is built around peer support, combining tracking with a community of women going through the same stage. If part of what you want is to feel less alone, Perry's community angle may matter more than any single feature; if you want conversational guidance alongside logging, Caria leans that way.

**Best for:** women who value AI guidance (Caria) or peer community (Perry) alongside tracking; feature sets change, so verify on each app's store listing.

## Clue — a science-forward tracker with a perimenopause mode

Clue is a long-running, science-forward period and cycle tracker with a strong privacy reputation, headquartered in Berlin and subject to strict EU data-protection law. It lets you track a wide range of symptoms and switch between modes, including perimenopause.

Per its makers, Clue's Perimenopause mode adds a cycle view that accounts for changing cycle lengths, alongside symptom tracking — a credible option if you already trust Clue and want to keep using it into midlife. The app grew up around reproductive-age cycles, though, so its center of gravity is still the cycle itself rather than predicting difficult days.

**Best for:** existing Clue users and anyone who prioritizes a privacy-first, science-forward tracker; perimenopause is one mode among several. See the [Clue perimenopause overview](https://helloclue.com/articles/menopause/introducing-clue-perimenopause).

## Flo — the largest cycle tracker, with new perimenopause features

Flo is the largest general period and cycle tracker, and it has added a dedicated perimenopause experience. According to Flo, this includes a Perimenopause Score — which the company describes as the first digital assessment tool validated for perimenopause symptoms — plus symptom tracking, an anonymous community, and a prediction window for your next period rather than a single date, which suits irregular cycles better.

Flo's scale means a polished, well-resourced app with a lot of content. As with Clue, its foundation is reproductive-age cycle tracking, so perimenopause is a layer on top rather than the original purpose — but if you already use Flo, a reasonable on-ramp.

**Best for:** current Flo users and those who want a large, well-funded app with broad content. Details are on [Flo's perimenopause announcement](https://flo.health/newsroom/flo-for-perimenopause-is-launching-to-empower-the-1-billion-women-who-experience-perimenopause-without-the-support-they-deserve).

## Natural Cycles and Apple Health — useful, but for different jobs

**Natural Cycles** is notable as the first birth-control app cleared by the FDA, back in 2018. It uses daily basal body temperature and cycle data to identify fertile days — a genuinely distinct, regulated use, but one built around ovulatory cycles rather than midlife symptoms. See its method on the [Natural Cycles science page](https://www.naturalcycles.com/the-science).

**Apple Health Cycle Tracking** is built into the iPhone and logs periods and some symptoms for free — a fine no-frills logbook, but with little perimenopause-specific intelligence: no prediction of hard days, no doctor-ready report.

**Best for:** Natural Cycles suits those who specifically want FDA-cleared, hormone-free contraception; Apple Health suits minimal free logging. Neither is built to interpret perimenopause symptom patterns.

## How to choose the right menopause app for you

A quick way to decide: to **anticipate hard days and bring a clear report to your doctor**, try a prediction-first app like Rythma; for **clinician-backed education**, Balance leads; for **community or AI guidance**, look at Perry or Caria; if you are **happy with a cycle tracker**, Clue and Flo both have perimenopause modes; if you need **FDA-cleared contraception**, that is Natural Cycles' lane; and for **free and minimal**, Apple Health logs the basics.

Most of these apps are free to download, so try one or two for a few weeks. Whatever you pick, the value comes from consistent logging — patterns only emerge once you have enough data.
