*These two come at midlife from opposite ends. Flo is the largest general period and cycle tracker, and its perimenopause experience includes — per the company — a Perimenopause Score it describes as the first digital assessment tool designed and validated specifically for perimenopause symptoms, tracking for hot flashes, mood, fatigue and sleep, a window-based next-period estimate rather than a single date, doctor-question guidance, and an anonymous in-app community. Balance, from Newson Health and founded by Dr. Louise Newson, is a menopause app built on symptom tracking, a large library of evidence-based articles, and a Health Report for appointments; per its makers it has been recognized by the NHS and certified by ORCHA. Flo is a consumer product that scaled into menopause; Balance is a clinical product that built an app. Choose by whether you want reach and peers or depth and authority.*

If you are weighing these two, you are usually deciding whether you want the polish and company of a mass-market app or the standing of a clinician-founded one. Both are reasonable; they are just not the same kind of thing.

We make a perimenopause app ourselves — Rythma — and note that where relevant at the end. Everything about Flo and Balance below comes from each company's own published materials.

## The stage they are being judged against

Perimenopause usually begins in the mid- to late 40s and lasts about four years on average, sometimes as long as eight, according to the U.S. Office on Women's Health. Periods may run longer or shorter, heavier or lighter, skip months entirely, and you may not ovulate every cycle. The Cleveland Clinic describes hormone levels going up and down "like a rollercoaster" rather than declining smoothly.

The symptom load is broad. In an international survey of more than 17,000 women across 158 countries analyzed by The Menopause Society, fatigue was the most reported symptom at 83%, ahead of irritability (80%), depressive mood (77%), and sleep problems (76%) — all outranking hot flashes at 71%.

Both apps track well past bleeding, so the real separators are what they add around the tracking.

## Flo — scale, assessment, and other women

Flo's advantages come from being enormous and well-resourced.

Its **Perimenopause Score** is the headline: the company describes it as the first digital assessment tool designed and validated specifically for perimenopause symptoms. Practically, it converts a general sense of feeling awful into a structured result you can reference at an appointment.

Its **window-based next-period estimate** is an honest design decision. Standard prediction averages recent cycle lengths, which stops working when those lengths destabilize — under the STRAW+10 staging system, the early menopause transition is defined by consecutive cycle lengths persistently differing by seven or more days, and the late transition by a stretch of 60 or more days without a period. Replacing a confident date with a range acknowledges that precision has gone.

Its **community** is a real asset. The most common symptoms of this stage are the least recognized as belonging to it, which makes it isolating in a specific way. Reading other women describe exactly what you are experiencing is not a minor feature.

**Best for:** a polished, well-resourced tracker with a structured assessment and peers to compare notes with.

**Trade-offs:** cycle-first by origin, so perimenopause is a layer on top; some features sit behind a paid tier; less depth of clinical content than Balance. See Flo's [perimenopause announcement](https://flo.health/newsroom/flo-for-perimenopause-is-launching-to-empower-the-1-billion-women-who-experience-perimenopause-without-the-support-they-deserve).

## Balance — depth, authorship, and a report

Balance's advantages come from clinical provenance rather than scale.

Its **article library** is the centre of the product, not an add-on, and the content carries named clinical authorship. Its **Health Report** is designed to be taken to appointments. And per its makers, Balance has been recognized by the NHS and certified by the digital-health assessor ORCHA.

What that buys is standing. If part of your problem is being dismissed — and for many women in this stage it is — arriving with a report from a clinician-founded, NHS-recognized product plus the reading to explain what you are asking about is a different negotiating position than arriving with an app screenshot.

**Best for:** women who want authoritative education and a document that helps them be taken seriously.

**Trade-offs:** broad menopause focus rather than perimenopause-specific prediction; some features are paid; no community; reading is slower than asking. See the [Balance app page](https://www.balance-menopause.com/balance-app/).

## Head-to-head

**Credibility.** Balance, clearly. Named clinical authorship and NHS recognition are a different class of signal from scale.

**Community.** Flo, clearly. Balance has no equivalent.

**Assessment.** Flo's Perimenopause Score is more structured than anything Balance offers as a scored instrument; Balance's Health Report is stronger as a document to hand over. They are different tools for the same appointment.

**Cycle handling.** Flo has done more visible work on prediction honesty with its window approach. Balance treats the cycle as one input among many midlife symptoms, which suits later-stage users better.

**Polish and habit.** Flo is the more refined consumer product, which matters more than it sounds — the tracker that helps is the one you actually open daily.

**The appointment.** Both help. Balance generates the report; Flo helps you know what to ask. Either way, logging is what lets you catch what the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists says warrants attention: very heavy bleeding (soaking a pad or tampon hourly for two or more hours), bleeding between periods or after sex, cycles staying consistently closer than about 21 days, or any bleeding after menopause.

**What neither does.** Neither forecasts which of your days are likely to be difficult. Flo assesses your overall symptom burden; Balance explains what is happening. Anticipation is a third capability — the one our own app, Rythma, is built around — and it is what lets you move a meeting off a bad day rather than understand the day afterward.

## How to choose

- **You want to be taken seriously and understand your options.** Balance.
- **You want peers and a structured assessment.** Flo.
- **You want a report to hand over.** Balance generates one.
- **You do not know what to ask for yet.** Flo's doctor-question framing.
- **You want your hard days forecast.** Neither — look at prediction-first apps.

Both are free to download with paid tiers, and using them together is coherent: Flo as the daily habit, Balance as the reference and report. Whichever you use, log consistently, because everything either offers depends on the data behind it.
