Perimenopause is a stage where two very different needs show up at once: understanding that you are not the only one experiencing this, and getting practical control over days that keep ambushing you. Perry and Rythma each answer one of those well.

## Why community matters so much in this stage

It is worth taking Perry's premise seriously before comparing anything, because it addresses a genuine problem.

Perimenopause is isolating in a specific way: its most common symptoms are its least recognized ones. In an international survey of more than 17,000 women across 158 countries analyzed by The Menopause Society, fatigue topped the list at 83%, ahead of irritability (80%), depressive mood (77%), and sleep problems (76%) — all outranking hot flashes, which only 71% even recognized as a perimenopause sign.

That gap is the whole problem. The symptoms most likely to affect your work and your relationships are the ones least likely to be attributed to perimenopause, by you or by anyone around you. Years of being told it is stress is a common story. When that is your experience, hearing another woman describe the exact same thing is not a soft benefit — it is the correction to being dismissed, and no feature list substitutes for it.

## Perry — built around peer community

Perry is a perimenopause app that combines symptom logging with a community of women in the same stage. Because it is designed for midlife rather than reproductive-age cycles, it is not fighting the assumptions that trip up standard period apps.

Its distinguishing feature is the community layer: shared experience, questions answered by people living the same thing, and the specific reassurance of "yes, me too." For someone whose main pain point is isolation or self-doubt about whether what they are feeling is real, that is the right tool.

Where Perry stops is forecasting. A community can tell you that skipped periods and 3am waking are normal for this stage; it cannot tell you which days *next week* are likely to be hard for you specifically. Perry's tracking and feature set continue to evolve, so check its own listing for current capabilities.

**Best for:** women who want peer community and shared experience alongside symptom tracking.

**Trade-offs:** community-first rather than prediction-first; verify current tracking features on its store listing.

## Rythma — built for prediction and a doctor-ready summary

Rythma is our app, so we will be concrete rather than complimentary. It is an iPhone app built for perimenopause from the start: rather than assuming a fixed monthly cycle, it expects irregularity and learns your individual symptom patterns over time.

Its distinguishing feature is prediction of difficult days. As you log, Rythma identifies your personal pattern and flags likely high-symptom stretches — fatigue, poor sleep, mood shifts, hot flashes, brain fog — before they arrive, so you can plan around them instead of being caught out.

The second piece is the doctor report: a structured, shareable summary of your symptoms and trends. Given how often midlife symptoms are attributed to stress, walking into an appointment with months of evidence rather than a recollection changes the conversation materially.

**Best for:** women whose periods have become irregular, who want to anticipate hard days and bring a clear summary to their doctor.

**Trade-offs:** iPhone-only today; prediction improves the more you log, so the first weeks are mostly data-gathering; and it is not community-first the way Perry is.

## Head-to-head: the honest differences

**Validation vs. anticipation.** Perry's answer to a bad week is other women telling you it is normal. Rythma's answer is having seen the bad week coming. Both are real forms of relief, and which one you need often depends on how long you have been in this. Early on, validation usually matters more.

**What each does with your logs.** Perry's tracking gives you a personal record and context from the community. Rythma's tracking feeds a model of your individual pattern that produces a forecast. That is a different use of the same input.

**The appointment.** Rythma generates a report designed to be handed over. Either app's log helps you spot the bleeding changes the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists says warrant medical attention — very heavy bleeding (soaking a pad or tampon hourly for two or more hours), bleeding between periods or after sex, or cycles staying consistently closer than about 21 days — but only one of them packages it for you.

**Irregularity.** Neither app assumes regularity, which is why both suit this stage. Under the STRAW+10 staging system, the early menopause transition is defined by consecutive cycle lengths persistently differing by seven or more days, and the late transition by a gap of 60 or more days. Both treat that as ordinary input.

**Platform.** Rythma is iPhone-only today. If you are on Android, check Perry's current availability — that may decide it for you.

- **You feel alone in this and want people who understand.** Perry.
