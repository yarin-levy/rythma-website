Flo is the largest general period and cycle tracker and now has a dedicated perimenopause experience: according to the company, it includes a Perimenopause Score described as the first digital assessment tool designed and validated specifically for perimenopause symptoms, tracking for hot flashes, mood changes, fatigue, and sleep, a window-based next-period estimate rather than a single date, guidance framed around questions for your doctor, and an anonymous in-app community. ## What Flo actually offers for perimenopause

Flo's perimenopause experience, per the company, includes:

- **A Perimenopause Score**, which Flo describes as the first digital assessment tool designed and validated specifically for perimenopause symptoms.
- **Symptom tracking** covering hot flashes, mood changes, fatigue, and sleep.
- **A window-based next-period prediction** rather than a single predicted date.
- **Guidance framed around questions to raise with your doctor.**
- **An anonymous in-app community.**

You can read Flo's own description in its [perimenopause announcement](https://flo.health/newsroom/flo-for-perimenopause-is-launching-to-empower-the-1-billion-women-who-experience-perimenopause-without-the-support-they-deserve).

## Where Flo genuinely helps in midlife

**The prediction window is an honest design choice.** Most trackers forecast your next period by averaging recent cycle lengths, which only works while those lengths are stable. Under the STRAW+10 staging system used by clinicians, the early menopause transition is defined by consecutive cycle lengths persistently differing by seven or more days, and the late transition by a stretch of 60 or more days without a period. Switching from a confident date to a range is Flo acknowledging that precision is no longer available — which is more honest than most apps manage.

**The assessment gives you something concrete.** A structured score turns "I have been feeling awful" into a documented result you can bring to an appointment. Given how routinely midlife symptoms get attributed to stress, that has practical value.

**The symptom coverage reaches past bleeding.** That matters because perimenopause does. In an international survey of more than 17,000 women across 158 countries analyzed by The Menopause Society, fatigue was the most reported symptom at 83%, ahead of irritability (80%), depressive mood (77%), and sleep problems (76%) — all outranking hot flashes at 71%. An app that only tracks periods would miss most of the transition.

**The community is a real benefit.** The symptoms most likely to disrupt your life in this stage are the ones least recognized as belonging to it. Reading other women describe the same thing is not a minor feature.

## Where Flo's cycle-first origin shows

**It centers the cycle, because that is what it was built around.** Even with a perimenopause mode, the app's organizing principle remains the menstrual cycle. In late perimenopause — where the U.S. Office on Women's Health notes you may skip months entirely and may not ovulate every cycle — the cycle becomes a weaker and weaker frame for organizing your experience.

**It does not forecast your hard days.** This is the main gap. A Perimenopause Score tells you how you are doing overall. Symptom logging tells you what happened. Neither tells you that Tuesday and Wednesday next week are likely to be rough for you specifically, based on your own history. That is a different capability, and it is the one that lets you plan.

**Some features sit behind a paid tier.** Check the current listing before assuming what is included.

**Hormones do not fluctuate on a schedule.** The Cleveland Clinic describes perimenopausal hormone levels as going up and down "like a rollercoaster," with estrogen swinging out of balance with progesterone rather than declining smoothly. Any tool anchored to cycle position is describing something less stable than it appears.

## Should you stay on Flo or switch?

A practical way to decide.

**Stay with Flo if:** you value the community, you want a structured assessment to take to your doctor, you are early in the transition and mostly want to understand what is happening, or you are on Android and want a well-resourced app with midlife features.

**Look elsewhere if:** your main need is anticipating difficult days rather than estimating your period; you want a report built for handing to a clinician; the cycle-centric framing has started to feel like it is describing someone else's body; or privacy is your leading concern, in which case Clue's Berlin base and EU data-protection posture are worth weighing.

**What "elsewhere" means.** Perimenopause-native apps approach midlife from different angles: Balance leads on clinician-backed education and a health report; Caria offers AI-assisted guidance; Perry is built around peer community; and Rythma — our app — is built around prediction, learning your personal symptom patterns to forecast difficult days and generating a doctor report. Each is solving a different problem, so the honest recommendation depends on which one is yours.

## What to do either way

Whatever app you use, the same habits determine whether it helps.

Log consistently rather than in bursts, because patterns need continuous data. Track the symptoms that actually disrupt your days — sleep, energy, mood, hot flashes — not only bleeding. Record intensity, not just occurrence. And give it several weeks before judging, since nothing useful emerges from a fortnight of scattered entries.

Also know when to stop consulting an app and call a doctor. The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists advises seeing a clinician for very heavy bleeding — soaking a pad or tampon every hour for two or more hours in a row — as well as bleeding between periods or after sex, cycles that stay consistently closer together than about 21 days, or any bleeding after menopause.
